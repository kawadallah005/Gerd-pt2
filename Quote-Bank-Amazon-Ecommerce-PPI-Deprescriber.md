## Drug Review Aggregators (functionally equivalent to product reviews for this avatar)

1. **(P)** Omeprazole holds an average patient rating of 4.7 out of 10 across 513 reviews on Drugs.com, with 32% of reviewers reporting a positive experience and 51% reporting a negative one. — drugs.com/comments/omeprazole, accessed 2026-09-13.

2. **(P)** Patient-volunteered side effects cluster around severe headaches, weakness/fatigue, body aches, jitteriness, fluttering heartbeat, anxiety, stomach pain, severe brain fog, heart pounding and racing, pins and needles in the hands and feet, chills, dizziness, lack of appetite, confusion, heart palpitations, tingling in the arms and legs, insomnia, and joint/arthritis-type body aches. — drugs.com/comments/omeprazole (pages 2-3) and askapatient.com/viewrating.asp?drug=76048, accessed 2026-09-13.

3. **(P)** At least one long-term patient reported being able to "eat anything with no side effects" after 30 years of continuous use, illustrating the genuinely wide variance in individual response this avatar has to weigh against their own negative experience. — drugs.com/comments/omeprazole, accessed 2026-09-13.

## Patient Testimonial / Health-Column Reader Comments (functioning as public product/treatment reviews)

6. **(V)** "I switched to omeprazole that I buy over the counter. When I read the label it said not to take it for more than 14 days in a row. As soon as I stopped, the pain in my stomach was unbearable. I have to keep taking it." — Reader comment, The People's Pharmacy, peoplespharmacy.com/articles/stopping-omeprazole-provoked-unbearable-pain, accessed 2026-09-13.

7. **(V)** "Over the course of a year I finally weaned myself off it. I now control my reflux very well by eating a very low-carb diet and taking DGL as well as famotidine when I do have symptoms." — Reader comment, The People's Pharmacy, accessed 2026-09-13.

8. **(NV)** Rocky Angelucci initially called Prilosec (omeprazole) "a miracle drug," but every attempt to stop over the next decade left him feeling worse than before; he described the withdrawal experience as "horrible burning, choking reflux" and a sensation like he had "swallowed a potato and it was stuck halfway down, along with burning acidity." He succeeded in quitting for good roughly eight years before this research was conducted. — The People's Pharmacy, peoplespharmacy.com/articles/getting-off-omeprazole, accessed 2026-09-13.

9. **(P)** A separate reader phased off omeprazole over seven months by gradually reducing the dose; the column notes that gradual tapering over roughly six weeks is the generally advised minimum approach. — The People's Pharmacy, accessed 2026-09-13.
