## Blogs / Independent Health Commentary

13. **(V)** "Treating a chronic disease is profitable, but curing it is a financial disaster." — Framing discussed (and contested) in a piece examining Big Pharma conspiracy theories, drhoffman.com/article/is-there-really-a-vast-conspiracy-to-deprive-us-of-cures-and-profit-off-our-misery, accessed 2026-09-13. Presented here as a belief this avatar may hold or encounter, not as an endorsed factual claim; the source itself is critical of the conspiracy framing even while documenting it.

14. **(P)** A more alternative-health-leaning source frames PPIs for GERD as "notorious for creating acid rebound," describing the mechanism where residual acid after stopping is enough to irritate the esophagus but insufficient to signal the lower esophageal sphincter to close, driving patients back to the medication. — baddoctors.news, "Reassessing acid reflux: Why low stomach acid, not excess, may be driving a $10 billion drug market," accessed 2026-09-13. Flagged explicitly as an alternative-health-oriented outlet, not a mainstream medical source; included because this avatar demonstrably encounters this framing in their own research.

15. **(P)** The "low stomach acid" (hypochlorhydria) counter-theory to standard GERD treatment is discussed across at least two independent health-commentary newsletters, with the caveat that it remains a debated, unproven theory for the general reflux population, and that chronic PPI use can itself eventually impair acid-secreting glands. — adamrinde.substack.com/p/could-my-stomach-acid-be-too-low and untreated.substack.com/p/the-acid-paradox, both accessed 2026-09-13; Cleveland Clinic's hypochlorhydria overview, accessed 2026-09-13.
